# GLOSSARY-AR — fixed Arabic renderings

The agreed Arabic term for every recurring concept. **One rendering each, used
everywhere.** Inconsistent terminology is the fastest way to look amateur, so this
file is the authority: A3 translation must match it exactly, no synonyms.

Voice target (from the brief): warm, plain, confident, contemporary Modern Standard
Arabic — the register a professional leadership brand uses. Not literary, not
dialect, not translated-brochure stiffness.

Status: **DRAFT** — awaiting owner review alongside the A1 sample. Where I chose
between two defensible terms, the runner-up and the reason are noted so the owner
can overrule with full context.

## Gender of address — PROJECT-WIDE DECISION NEEDED

Arabic verbs and pronouns are gendered; English "you" and generic "coach" are not.
Every imperative in the questions ("Write…", "Choose…", "Ask…") and every agent
noun ("coach", "colleague") forces a masculine/feminine choice.

Three options:
1. **Masculine generic** — «اكتب، اختَر، اسأل». Conventional default in MSA
   professional copy; cleanest to read; but literally addresses a man.
2. **Dual address** — «اكتب/اكتبي». Inclusive but visually cluttered, especially
   in the 50 step lines and form labels. Reads like a form, not a brand.
3. **Gender-neutral rephrasing** — recast imperatives as nouns/infinitives where
   possible («البدء بـ…» instead of «ابدأ بـ…»). Cleanest where it works, but many
   imperatives have no natural neutral form.

**The A1 sample below uses masculine generic (option 1)** as the conventional
default, so the owner can judge the *voice* without the gender question tangling it.
This is a placeholder choice, not a recommendation — **owner decides before A3.**
Given the founder is a woman and the audience is mixed, this deserves a deliberate
call. Recorded in docs/DECISIONS.md once chosen.
